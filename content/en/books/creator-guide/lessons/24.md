# 24 Novels and notes: the kind field

> Not every book needs questions.

## Why the field exists

The format began life assuming textbooks, and so it enforced: one in-class plus two quiz questions per lesson, and an error if a lesson had none.

But people want to use the same format for **novels** and for **notes**. Those have no questions, and the hard rule made them impossible.

Hence `kind`:

```
"kind": "textbook"    default; question counts enforced
"kind": "novel"       no question requirement
"kind": "notes"       no question requirement
"kind": "other"       anything else
```

## Omit it and you get the strict rules

`kind` is optional. Leave it out and you get `textbook`, i.e. **the strict checks**.

## What relaxes for non-textbooks

| Check | Textbook | Novel / notes |
|---|---|---|
| At least one question per lesson | required | not required |
| 2 exam questions per lesson | required | not required |
| At least 5 in a chapter test | warning | not required |
| Matching question counts across languages | required | not required |
| **Are the questions themselves valid** | checked | **still checked** |

That last row is the point: **counts are optional, validity is not.**

An out-of-range `answer` or a space-separated `cases` still errors in a novel. If you include a question, it has to be gradeable.

## Unknown values are treated leniently

Misspell `kind` (say `textbok`) and you get a warning plus **lenient** handling.

Deliberate: better to let a strange book through than to block a real textbook over a typo.

## What a novel's contents look like

```
content/en/lessons/01.md    chapter one
content/en/lessons/02.md    chapter two
```

One lesson is one chapter, with no chapter tests (just leave `test` out of toc).

## Recap

- `kind` decides whether question counts are enforced
- Omit it and you get textbook strictness
- Non-textbooks still need any included questions to be valid
