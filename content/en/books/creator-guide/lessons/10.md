# 10 How long should a lesson be

> Too short and it teaches nothing; too long and nobody finishes.

## A useful range

**60 to 150 lines** of body is comfortable.

Under 60, the validator says "might be placeholder content" — only a warning, but it usually means you are not finished yet.

Over 150, readers drift away. If a topic really is that big, **split it into two lessons** rather than cramming one.

## How to tell whether to split

One signal: **how many `##` subheadings does it have?**

```
2 ~ 4     right
5 or more consider splitting
1         probably too thin, or you forgot a section
```

## One lesson, one problem

Good titles:

```
Slicing: taking a whole span at once
Dictionaries: look up by key, not by position
Why 0.1 + 0.2 is not 0.3
```

Bad titles:

```
Lists and dictionaries and sets     three topics, split it
Python data structures (part 1)     "part 1" means you have not found the seam
Some handy functions                too scattered
```

## Do not warm up for too long

The first three screens decide whether a reader stays. Give them: what this lesson solves, an example they can follow immediately, and why they need it.

**Do not** open with two screens of history, design philosophy, or comparisons to other languages. Nobody cares about those until they can use the thing.

## Recap

- 60 to 150 lines
- One problem per lesson, and title it specifically
- An example within the first three screens
