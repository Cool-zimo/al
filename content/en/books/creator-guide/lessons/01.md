# 01 What a book is made of

> A book is just a repo. The layout is simpler than you think.

## Three kinds of files, and that is it

At its smallest, an AnyLearn book has only three things:

```
my-book/
├── albook.json          identity: title, author, languages
├── README.md            human-readable notes (optional, but please)
└── content/
    ├── en/
    │   ├── toc.json     table of contents
    │   └── lessons/
    │       ├── 01.md
    │       ├── 02.md
    │       └── test-01.md
    └── zh/
        ├── toc.json
        └── lessons/
            ├── 01.md
            └── test-01.md
```

**albook.json is the entry point.** The bot looks for this file at the repo root to decide "is this an al-book or not".

**content/ is split by language.** English goes in `content/en/`, Chinese in `content/zh/`. Writing only one language? Then you have one directory. That is completely fine.

**lessons/ holds the lessons.** The file name *is* the lesson number: `01.md`, `02.md`… Chapter tests are `test-01.md`.

## Chapters and lessons

Five lessons make a chapter, and each chapter ends with a test. Not a hard rule, but following it saves you a lot of thinking:

| Files | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `01.md` ~ `05.md` | the 5 lessons of chapter 1 |
| `test-01.md` | chapter 1 test |
| `06.md` ~ `10.md` | the 5 lessons of chapter 2 |
| `test-02.md` | chapter 2 test |

A chapter's **title** does not live in the file name. It lives in `toc.json`.

## Do not pile files at the root

People try putting lessons at the repo root, or nesting them per chapter (`ch1/01.md`). **Neither works.** The reader only knows one path: `content/{language}/lessons/{number}.md`.

Add one extra directory level and the whole book becomes unreadable.

## Recap

- `albook.json` must sit at the root
- lessons always live under `content/{language}/lessons/`
- lesson numbers are two digits; chapter tests are `test-NN`
- chapter titles go in toc, not in file names
