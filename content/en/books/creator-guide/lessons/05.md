# 05 Handling two languages

> Start with one language. Do not let bilingual scare you off.

## Two directories, fully independent

```
content/
├── zh/
│   ├── toc.json
│   └── lessons/ 01.md ... 30.md, test-01.md ...
└── en/
    ├── toc.json
    └── lessons/ 01.md ... 30.md, test-01.md ...
```

These are **two independent sets of files**. You declare which ones you wrote with `langs` in `albook.json`:

```
"langs": ["zh"]        Chinese only
"langs": ["en"]        English only
"langs": ["zh","en"]   both
```

Declare `en` with an empty `content/en/` and you get an error. The reverse — files present but not declared — is not an error, they simply do not show.

## Two hard rules for bilingual books

The site cross-checks both sides:

1. **Lesson counts must match** — 30 in Chinese means 30 in English
2. **Question counts must match** — 3 per lesson in Chinese means 3 in English

Rule 2 applies only to **textbooks**. Writing a novel (`kind: novel`)? Question counts are not checked — finishing one language first is normal.

## Do not machine-translate

Worth saying on its own: **rewrite the English properly; do not run it through a translator.**

Readers spot machine-translated lessons instantly. The sentences are grammatical but awkward, and the examples still carry Chinese names, currency and places.

Rewrite with a **different setting**: where Chinese says "Xiao Ming bought three jin of potatoes at the market", the English should read "Alice went to the market and bought three pounds of potatoes" — not "Xiao Ming".

## One language first is fine

Plenty of books are written in Chinese first and translated months later. Just set `langs` to `["zh"]`, then change it to `["zh","en"]` and republish when the English is ready.

## Recap

- Two independent trees, declared via `langs`
- Lesson counts must match; question counts only for textbooks
- Rewrite, do not translate
- Starting with one language is fine
